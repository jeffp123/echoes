Echoes vs a journal, a habit tracker, a to-do list, or a notes app

Updated October 27, 2026  ·  Jeff Peck

Echoes is easy to mistake for a journal, a habit tracker, a to-do list, or a notes app. It's none of them. Here's the difference, and who each one is for.

A journal captures. Echoes resurfaces.

A journal is built for writing things down. What you wrote goes into an archive, and most of it stays there. The insight you had in March is technically saved, but you won't see it again unless you go looking.

Echoes works the other way around. You write fewer things, and they come back to you. Each Echo is something worth remembering: a thing you have learned, a quote that has stayed with you, a value you want to keep close. Each morning, Echoes selects a short list from your library, and you see a few of your own words again.

If you want a place to write every day, use a journal. If you already know what you want to remember and keep forgetting it, that's what Echoes is for.

A habit tracker counts. Echoes doesn't.

Habit trackers run on streaks, scores, and history. That works for some people. For others, one missed day turns the app into a source of guilt, and then it gets deleted.

Echoes has none of that. Missing a day doesn't break anything. There's no score, no history, no guilt. Yesterday's list is gone. The app doesn't track what you've done or how often. You tap an Echo when you've read it, thought about it, or just acknowledged it. That's the whole interaction.

If you want to be held accountable to a daily number, a habit tracker is the right tool. If you want a calm daily moment with nothing to keep up, Echoes is.

A to-do list finishes tasks. Echoes keeps things close.

Apps like Todoist and Apple Reminders are built for things you do once and then finish. Check off "renew passport" and it's gone for good.

An Echo is never finished. "When you feel impatient, slow down" isn't a task you complete, it's something you want to keep running into. So in Echoes, checking something off only means you saw it today. It stays in your library, and it comes back as often as you told it to.

If you want to get things done and cleared, use a to-do list. If you want the same few ideas to keep coming back, that's what Echoes is for.

A notes app stores. Echoes brings things back.

Apple Notes is a good place to put a quote or an idea you want to keep. But a note only does something when you open it, and most notes don't get opened again.

Readwise goes a step further and brings back highlights from books and articles you've read. Echoes is closer to that, with one difference: every Echo is something you wrote yourself, in your own words, not a passage you saved from someone else.

If you want an archive to search, use a notes app. If you want your own words in front of you each morning, that's what Echoes is for.

Frequency, not scheduling

Reminder apps ask you when. Echoes asks how often. You set each Echo to appear Occasionally, Now and then, Every few days, Often, or Daily, and the algorithm handles the rest. An Echo you haven't seen in a while is more likely to show up today.

At a glance

  • Journal: write a lot, archive it, look back when you choose.
  • Habit tracker: do the thing, keep the streak, watch the score.
  • To-do list: check it off, and it's done for good.
  • Notes app: save it, and find it again when you search.
  • Echoes: write a few things in your own words, see a short list of them each morning, check them off. Nothing tracked.
